= Names and addresses
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

These rules cover how a product asks for, stores and shows the names of people and their postal addresses.

== Personal names

[[LOC-050]]
*LOC-050.* A product MUST NOT assume that a name has a first name and a last name.
It SHOULD ask for the full name in one field.
It MAY ask for separate parts only when a process needs them, such as a match with another register.
Those parts MUST NOT be mandatory, and their labels SHOULD read "Given names" and "Family name".
Many people have one name only, several family names, or the family name first.
A form built for one pattern rejects the others.

[[LOC-051]]
*LOC-051.* A name field MUST accept any link:https://www.unicode.org/standard/standard.html[Unicode] letter and diacritical mark.
It MUST also accept spaces, hyphens and apostrophes.
It MUST accept a name of one character and SHOULD allow at least 100 characters.
A product MUST store and show the name as the person wrote it, with no change of case.
Names such as "D'Amours", "Lévesque-Côté" or "McNeil" lose their meaning when a system rewrites them.

[[LOC-052]]
*LOC-052.* A product MUST NOT require a title, such as "Mr." or "Ms.".
It SHOULD ask separately for the name that the person wants people to use.
A title reveals a gender that the service may not need.
The name used every day can differ from the legal name.

== Postal addresses

[[LOC-053]]
*LOC-053.* A product MUST store a postal address as separate fields, with the country as an link:https://www.iso.org/iso-3166-country-codes.html[ISO 3166-1] two-letter code.
It MUST show and print the address in the format of its country.
The fields MUST fit the country.
A product MUST NOT require a postal code or a region where the country has none.
Each country orders and names the parts of an address in its own way.
Separate fields let a product print one address for many postal systems.

[[LOC-054]]
*LOC-054.* A product MUST format a Canadian address as the link:https://www.canadapost-postescanada.ca/cpc/en/support/articles/addressing-guidelines/civic-address.page[addressing guidelines] of Canada Post describe.
The municipality, the two-letter code of the province or territory and the postal code go on the same line.
The postal code MUST be in capital letters, in the form `A1A 1A1`, with one space.
A mailing label MAY use capital letters and no punctuation, as Canada Post recommends.
Mail sorting machines read addresses in this format.

[[LOC-055]]
*LOC-055.* A product MUST NOT translate the names of streets or municipalities.
It MUST keep an address in its original language, and SHOULD mark that language.
`Trois-Rivières` stays `Trois-Rivières` on an English screen.
A street such as `rue Principale` keeps its French order and type.
Official place names have one form, and a translated name can send mail to the wrong place.
