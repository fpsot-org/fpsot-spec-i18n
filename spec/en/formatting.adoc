= Formatting
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

These rules cover how a product shows a date, a number or an amount to a person.
They apply to display only.
Programs exchange values in forms that do not depend on a {term-locale}, such as {abbr-rfc-first} 3339 dates and times.

== Locale data

[[LOC-010]]
*LOC-010.* A product MUST format text for display with the data of the {abbr-cldr-first}.
This covers dates and times, numbers and percentages, currency amounts, units and lists.
It MUST use a library that reads this data, such as {abbr-icu-first} or the formatting classes of the platform.
A product MUST NOT write its own date or number patterns for a locale.
The same amount reads `$1,234.56` in `en-CA` and `1 234,56 $` in `fr-CA`.
Hand-written patterns get such details wrong and drift from the shared data.

[[LOC-011]]
*LOC-011.* A product SHOULD use {abbr-cldr} 49 or later.
It MUST state its {abbr-cldr} version in its release notes.
Tests SHOULD compare formatted text with the output of the library, not with strings typed by hand.
Each {abbr-cldr} version fixes and changes some formats, such as the space characters inside numbers.
A stated version lets a reviewer explain a change in the output.

== Dates, times and amounts

[[LOC-012]]
*LOC-012.* A product MUST show a date and time in the time zone that the reader expects.
This is the zone of the person, or of the place that the value belongs to.
It MUST name that zone with a name from the time zone database of the {abbr-iana-first}, such as `America/Toronto`.
It SHOULD show the zone next to the time when readers may live in other zones.
An offset alone does not follow daylight saving time.
A deadline shown without its zone misleads a person in another province.

[[LOC-013]]
*LOC-013.* A product MUST take the currency of an amount from the data, never from the locale of the person.
A `fr-CA` reader who looks at an amount in United States dollars sees `1 234,56 $ US`.
A locale sets how to write an amount, not which currency it is in.

[[LOC-014]]
*LOC-014.* A product MUST NOT read formatted text back as data.
A date or number field MUST turn the entry into a value free of locale conventions.
It does so before it stores or sends that value.
The comma is a decimal sign in French and a group sign in English.
Text formatted for one reader would mean something else to another program.

== Sorting

[[LOC-015]]
*LOC-015.* A product MUST sort text for people with the collation rules of the {abbr-cldr} for their locale.
It MUST NOT sort such text by code point or by byte.
In French, a word that starts with `é` belongs with the words in `e`, not after `z`.
Readers expect the order of their own dictionary.
